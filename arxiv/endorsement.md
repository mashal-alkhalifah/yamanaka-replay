# arXiv endorsement: candidates and the request

A first submission to `q-bio.QM` needs an endorsement from an arXiv author
who has submitted **two or more papers to any `q-bio.*` class between three
months and five years ago**. That window is, as of 22 October 2026,
submissions dated between **22 October 2021 and 22 July 2026**.

`q-bio.QM` is the lowest bar of the plausible archives — `cs.LG` asks the
endorser for three papers — so it stays the primary, with `stat.ME` and
`cs.LG` as cross-lists.

The endorsement code arXiv mails to the account is account-specific and is
deliberately **not** recorded in this public repository. Paste it into the
letter where the placeholder sits.

Each candidate below was checked against the arXiv API on 22 October 2026
(`au:"<name>" AND cat:q-bio.*`, sorted by submission date). To confirm any
of them independently, open one of their arXiv papers and follow the
"Which of the authors of this article can endorse?" link at the foot of the
abstract page.

## The letter

Short, specific, and honest about what is being asked. An endorsement is a
statement that the paper looks like it belongs in the archive, not a
judgement of its merit, and saying so plainly makes it easier to grant.

### English

> Subject: arXiv endorsement request for q-bio.QM
>
> Dear Professor <name>,
>
> I am an assistant research professor at the Institute of Smart Cities,
> King Abdulaziz City for Science and Technology (KACST), in Riyadh. I
> would like to post my first arXiv preprint to q-bio.QM and, having no
> prior arXiv submissions, I need an endorsement. arXiv's records show you
> are eligible to give one.
>
> The paper is *Factor-combination discovery is stochastic threshold group
> testing: a counterfactual replay of the 2006 reprogramming screen*. It
> casts the search for a small combination of transcription factors as
> stochastic threshold group testing and replays the Yamanaka screen under
> that model, measuring cost in wet-lab assays rather than compute. Its
> main result is a negative one: audited against the Norman and Joung
> perturbation datasets, the gap response the designer assumes is 0.10
> where the measurements say 0.77, and re-run on the measured structure the
> designer recovers 2.5% of recipes while reporting 0.991 mean confidence
> on its failures. Carrying that parameter as a latent variable instead of
> calibrating it restores 99.3%.
>
> [For Prof. Bunne: Your 2024 virtual-cell paper is cited in the
> discussion; this is a concrete, measured instance of the calibration
> problem it raises.]
>
> The work is computational throughout and makes no biological claim. The
> code, the per-run results and the full statistical report are public:
> https://github.com/mashal-alkhalifah/yamanaka-replay
> and the archived version has DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23436007. The manuscript
> itself is attached so you can see exactly what you would be endorsing.
>
> If you are willing, the endorsement can be entered at
> https://arxiv.org/auth/endorse with the code <CODE>.
>
> I understand an endorsement says the submission belongs in the archive
> and is not a judgement on the work, and I would not want it on any other
> basis. Thank you for considering it either way.
>
> With respect,
> Dr Mashal Saleh Alkhalifah
> Assistant Research Professor, Institute of Smart Cities
> King Abdulaziz City for Science and Technology, Riyadh
> mkhalifah@kacst.gov.sa

## Practical notes

- Attach `paper/main.pdf`. An endorser who can see the paper decides in one
  minute; one who cannot, does not reply.
- Ask one person at a time and wait about a week. Several requests arriving
  at once from the same author reads badly, and only one endorsement is
  needed.
- The code is archive-specific. Checking a second archive in the arXiv
  interface generates a second code and a second email; ignore the extra.
- Submission 8244875 is saved on arXiv and resumes once the endorsement
  lands. Nothing has to be re-entered.
